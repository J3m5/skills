# Write and publish

## Choose the support and audience

Read only the reference relevant to the requested deliverable. Links are routes,
not a reading checklist.

- PR/MR descriptions: [merge requests](references/merge-requests.md), including
  section order, related tickets and PRs/MRs, and concise validation.
- Comments on a code review: [review comments](references/review-comments.md).
- Requested QA/product ticket comments or handoffs:
  [QA handoffs](references/qa-handoffs.md).
- Short messages and emails: apply the shared guidance directly. Add structure
  only when it helps the reader.
- When asked to infer or refine the user's writing style:
  [authentic writing](references/style-evidence.md). Ordinary drafting does not
  require collecting new style samples.

Follow explicit user choices and the destination's required template. Keep
format-specific instructions in their reference rather than applying them to
other supports.

## Shared writing guidance

- Lead with the point and the action or decision it calls for. Keep information
  that changes the reader's understanding, decision, or next action. Remove
  repetition, process narration, and automatic summaries.
- Distinguish facts from expectations. State established behavior directly.
  Reserve "doit", "doivent", or "devrait" for obligations or recommendations.
- Use everyday language suited to the audience. Keep familiar technical terms
  and exact identifiers. Explain an unfamiliar concept briefly when needed.
- Account for what a regular collaborator already knows. Omit familiar
  architecture explanations, obvious prerequisites, and repeated reminders
  about known resource constraints unless they change the next action.
- Be concise without losing the affected behavior, useful evidence, or material
  limitations. Include tests, revisions, or environments when relevant to the
  deliverable, without appending an exhaustive validation block to every message.
- For French drafts addressed to colleagues, use a natural tone and complete
  sentences. Correct typing mistakes. Describe what happens and why, with a
  concrete request or next action when the reader needs one.
- Follow the author's punctuation preferences and any house style. By default,
  prefer full stops, commas, parentheses, and connecting words in prose and
  headings. Preserve required syntax in code, commands, URLs, and verbatim
  quotations. Check the title and body during drafting.

## Preserve approval and publish faithfully

- When proposing text for approval, show the complete publication-ready body
  separately from your own notes. Retain it as the authoritative payload.
- An approval such as "OK, post it" applies to that exact text and destination.
  Send the retained payload directly through a tool argument or body file.
  Preserve wording, punctuation, and formatting. Do not add context, checks,
  links, headings, or a signature after approval.
- Verifying the destination or current facts does not authorize changing the
  approved text. If it has become inaccurate, explain the issue and propose
  replacement text for approval before publishing.
- If the approved body cannot be identified, ask which text to publish instead
  of reconstructing it from a report or conversation summary.
- A direct instruction to draft and publish, or to make specified edits and
  publish, already authorizes that work. Apply the requested edits without
  unrelated rewriting or another approval step. Drafting alone does not
  authorize sending.
- After publication, read back the content and verify it matches the authorized
  body. If the outcome is uncertain, inspect the destination before retrying to
  avoid duplicate messages. Report an unverified outcome if it cannot be checked.
